skeleton_metrics.py — Skeleton-Based Comparison Metrics
========================================================

This file contains the core pose-comparison logic. It measures how closely a
student's body pose matches the teacher's by comparing joint angles and
center of gravity across all DTW-aligned frames.

The file has one public orchestrator (compute_skeleton_score) and four
private helpers: two "compute" functions (extract a numeric representation
from raw landmarks) and two "compare" functions (score the similarity
between teacher and student representations).


================================================================================
Function ordering and visibility
================================================================================

  compute_skeleton_score   public    Orchestrator — called from comparator.py
  _compute_joint_angles    private   Compute angle arrays from landmarks
  _compute_cog             private   Compute center of gravity from landmarks
  _compare_angles          private   Score angle similarity with decay
  _compare_cog             private   Score CoG similarity with decay


================================================================================
compute_skeleton_score — orchestrator
================================================================================

Purpose: Single entry point for all skeleton comparison logic. Called from
comparator.py with aligned teacher/student landmarks and a config object.

Step by step:

  1. Compute joint angles for both teacher and student via _compute_joint_angles.
  2. Score angle similarity via _compare_angles → angle_score, per_joint, worst.
  3. Compute center of gravity for both via _compute_cog.
  4. Score CoG similarity via _compare_cog → cog_score.
  5. Weighted combination:
       skeleton_score = 0.80 * angle_score + 0.20 * cog_score
     (weights from config.weight_angles and config.weight_cog)

Returns a dict with:
  - 'score': combined skeleton score (0-100)
  - 'per_joint_scores': dict of joint name → score
  - 'worst_frames': list of worst (frame, joint, error) tuples

This mirrors the pattern used by MaskMetrics.compute_mask_score — both
return a dict and keep all internal wiring private. Comparator.py only
needs one call per metric type.


================================================================================
1. _compute_joint_angles
================================================================================

Purpose: Convert raw landmark positions into meaningful joint angles.

Input:
  - landmarks: shape (N, 33, 4) — N frames, 33 MediaPipe landmarks, 4 values
    per landmark (x, y, z, visibility).
  - angle_definitions: tuple of (parent, joint, child) index triplets. Each
    triplet defines one angle to measure. The indices refer to MediaPipe
    landmark numbers (e.g. 11 = left shoulder, 13 = left elbow, 15 = left wrist).

Output:
  - angles: shape (N, num_angles) — one angle in degrees per joint per frame.

Step by step:

  1. Strip to 2D by taking only x and y columns:
       positions = landmarks[:, :, :2]          → shape (N, 33, 2)
     We discard z because the comparison is done in screen-space 2D. The z
     coordinate from MediaPipe is a relative depth estimate and is less
     reliable than x/y.

  2. For each angle triplet (parent, joint, child):

     a) Compute two vectors originating at the vertex "joint":
          vec_a = positions[:, parent, :] - positions[:, joint, :]
          vec_b = positions[:, child, :]  - positions[:, joint, :]
        Both have shape (N, 2) — one 2D vector per frame.

     b) Compute the dot product of vec_a and vec_b:
          dot = (vec_a * vec_b).sum(axis=-1)      → shape (N,)
        Element-wise multiply gives (N, 2), summing the last axis collapses
        to (N,). This is equivalent to a·b = ax*bx + ay*by.

     c) Compute the magnitudes (lengths) of both vectors:
          mag_a = norm(vec_a)                      → shape (N,)
          mag_b = norm(vec_b)                      → shape (N,)

     d) Guard against division by zero:
          denom = mag_a * mag_b
          denom = where(denom == 0, 1e-8, denom)
        If a landmark sits exactly on the joint (zero-length vector), the
        denominator would be zero. Replacing with a tiny epsilon avoids NaN.

     e) Compute the angle via the dot-product formula:
          cos(θ) = (a · b) / (|a| * |b|)
        Clip to [-1, 1] before arccos to handle floating-point overshoot,
        then convert radians to degrees.

  The result is a (N, 16) array when using the default 16 joint angle
  definitions from ComparisonConfig.

Current joint angle definitions (from comparison_config.py):
  Index  Triplet         Name                  What it measures
  ─────  ──────────────  ────────────────────  ──────────────────────────────
   0     (11, 13, 15)    left_elbow            Bend of the left arm
   1     (12, 14, 16)    right_elbow           Bend of the right arm
   2     (23, 25, 27)    left_knee             Bend of the left leg
   3     (24, 26, 28)    right_knee            Bend of the right leg
   4     (13, 11, 23)    left_shoulder         Left arm raise angle
   5     (14, 12, 24)    right_shoulder        Right arm raise angle
   6     (11, 23, 25)    left_hip              Left leg raise angle
   7     (12, 24, 26)    right_hip             Right leg raise angle
   8     (13, 15, 19)    left_wrist            Left hand bend/flex
   9     (14, 16, 20)    right_wrist           Right hand bend/flex
  10     (25, 27, 31)    left_ankle            Left foot flex
  11     (26, 28, 32)    right_ankle           Right foot flex
  12     (12, 11, 23)    left_inner_shoulder   Left side of chest opening
  13     (11, 12, 24)    right_inner_shoulder  Right side of chest opening
  14     (11, 23, 24)    left_inner_hip        Left side of pelvis tilt
  15     (12, 24, 23)    right_inner_hip       Right side of pelvis tilt

The "inner" angles capture torso shape — how the chest opens/closes and how
the pelvis tilts side to side. These are important for detecting upper-body
lean and hip sway that the outer joint angles would miss.


================================================================================
2. _compute_cog
================================================================================

Purpose: Estimate the body's center of gravity (CoG) per frame using a
weighted average of selected joint positions.

Input:
  - landmarks: shape (N, 33, 4)
  - cog_weights: dict mapping landmark index → body-segment weight.

Output:
  - cog: shape (N, 2) — (x, y) center of gravity per frame.

Step by step:

  1. Strip to 2D positions: shape (N, 33, 2).

  2. Extract joint indices and their weights from the dict:
       joint_indices = [0, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 23, 24, 25, 26, 27, 28]
       weights = [0.08, 0.06, 0.06, 0.03, 0.03, 0.02, 0.02,
                  0.15, 0.15, 0.06, 0.06, 0.02, 0.02]
     These approximate real biomechanical segment masses. The hips (23, 24)
     have the highest weight (0.15 each) because the pelvis region holds the
     most body mass. The head (0) gets 0.08. Extremities (wrists, ankles)
     get small weights.

  3. Select only the relevant landmark positions:
       selected = positions[:, joint_indices, :]     → shape (N, 13, 2)

  4. Compute weighted average using Einstein summation:
       cog = einsum('j,njd->nd', weights, selected) / weight_sum

     What einsum does here:
       - 'j' indexes the 13 joints
       - 'njd' is the selected positions: n=frame, j=joint, d=xy dimension
       - 'nd' is the output: n=frame, d=xy dimension
       - For each frame n and dimension d, it computes:
           sum over j of (weights[j] * selected[n, j, d])
       - Dividing by weight_sum (0.76) normalizes because the weights don't
         sum to 1.0 — they represent relative proportions of total body mass
         covered by these 13 landmarks (not all 33).

  The result is a single (x, y) point per frame representing where the
  body's mass center is. This tracks overall body position and sway.


================================================================================
3. _compare_angles
================================================================================

Purpose: Score how well the student's joint angles match the teacher's,
with per-joint tolerance zones and exponential decay penalties.

Input:
  - teacher_angles: shape (N, J) — angles in degrees (DTW-aligned).
  - student_angles: shape (N, J) — same shape, same alignment.
  - tolerances: dict of joint name → tolerance in degrees.
  - sigma: decay parameter (configured as angle_sigma = 35.0).

Output:
  - overall_score: float 0-100.
  - per_joint_scores: dict of joint name → score 0-100.
  - worst_frames: list of up to 5 (frame_idx, joint_name, error_degrees) tuples.

Scoring formula:

  For each frame and each joint:
    error = |teacher_angle - student_angle|

    If error <= tolerance:
      score = 100  (perfect — within acceptable range)

    If error > tolerance:
      excess = error - tolerance
      score = 100 * exp(-(excess / sigma)²)

  This is a Gaussian-shaped decay. The tolerance creates a "free zone" where
  small differences don't penalize at all. Beyond that, the penalty grows
  smoothly. With sigma = 35.0:
    - 35° beyond tolerance → score ≈ 37%
    - 50° beyond tolerance → score ≈ 12%
    - 70° beyond tolerance → score ≈ 2%

  The tolerances differ per joint type:
    - Elbows/knees: 15° (moderate — natural variance in limb bending)
    - Shoulders/hips/inner joints: 10° (tighter — torso pose is more stable)
    - Wrists/ankles: 20° (looser — extremities are noisy and less important)

Step by step:

  1. Compute absolute error matrix: shape (N, J).

  2. For each joint j, compute excess beyond tolerance, then apply the
     Gaussian decay formula. Results go into frame_scores: shape (N, J).

  3. Per-joint scores: average each column across all frames.

  4. Overall score: average of the entire (N, J) matrix — this weights all
     joints equally and all frames equally.

  5. Worst frames detection:
     a) Compute mean score per frame across all joints → shape (N,).
     b) Sort ascending (lowest scores = worst frames).
     c) Take the top 5 worst frames.
     d) For each worst frame, find the joint with the highest absolute error
        using argmax on the error row.
     e) Return (frame_index, joint_name, error_in_degrees) for each.

  These worst frames are used by the feedback system to tell the student
  exactly when and where they deviated most.


================================================================================
4. _compare_cog
================================================================================

Purpose: Score how closely the student's center of gravity tracks the
teacher's across the dance.

Input:
  - teacher_cog: shape (N, 2) — (x, y) per frame (DTW-aligned).
  - student_cog: shape (N, 2) — same shape, same alignment.
  - sigma: decay parameter (configured as cog_sigma = 0.08).

Output:
  - score: float 0-100.

Step by step:

  1. Compute Euclidean distance per frame:
       distances = norm(teacher_cog - student_cog, axis=-1)    → shape (N,)
     This is the straight-line distance between the two CoG points in
     normalized [0, 1] coordinate space.

  2. Apply Gaussian decay (no tolerance zone — any deviation is penalized):
       frame_scores = 100 * exp(-(distances / sigma)²)

     With sigma = 0.08 (8% of frame width):
       - 0.04 apart (4% of frame) → score ≈ 78%
       - 0.08 apart (8% of frame) → score ≈ 37%
       - 0.16 apart (16% of frame) → score ≈ 2%

     This is intentionally sensitive. The CoG should track closely — if the
     teacher leans left and the student doesn't, even a small spatial offset
     indicates a significant posture difference.

  3. Average all frame scores to get the final CoG score.

  Note: Unlike _compare_angles, there is no tolerance zone here. CoG
  comparison has no "free" error margin because the center of mass is a
  single aggregated metric, not a noisy per-joint measurement. Any shift
  indicates a real difference in body positioning.


================================================================================
How this fits into comparator.py
================================================================================

Comparator calls a single method:

  skeleton_result = SkeletonMetrics.compute_skeleton_score(
      aligned_teacher_lm, aligned_student_lm, config,
  )

All internal wiring (angles, CoG, weighting) is hidden. Comparator
accesses the results via dict keys:

  skeleton_result['score']            → combined 0-100 score
  skeleton_result['per_joint_scores'] → per-joint breakdown
  skeleton_result['worst_frames']     → worst moments for feedback

The skeleton score then feeds into the final weighted overall score
along with mask and trajectory scores.
